# Dynamic pass-through continuation

Branch: `experiment/pendulum_guided_rotation`. All prior checkpoints, policies,
physical models, reports and hold-gate results are preserved. New results are
`output/dynamic_pass_through_v1`; pilot preparation is
`output/dynamic_pilot_preparation_v1`. No PPO environment was rebuilt or changed.

## Decision

**The 45° stationary hold is inappropriate as a dynamic intermediate gate.**
It has been replaced only in this new analysis, not retroactively in previous
results. However, **NO-GO for Molab PPO at present**: greater collision-free body
rotation is feasible, but front support and terminal recovery fail. PPO and BC
updates in this continuation are both **zero**.

The new seed-42 dynamic sequence crosses 45° collision-free in **10/10 fresh
resets**, reaches >=50° in **8/10**, and peaks at **51.11° ± 1.23°** across those
resets. This is real offline joint-action progression, not a trained policy.
It loses front support for **.334–.348 s**, displaces the front feet by
**.669–.717 m**, and ends with body pitch velocity **−2.96 to −2.32 rad/s**.
None of these trials demonstrates terminal recovery. Therefore these saved
trajectories are development data, not approved imitation targets.

## Revised criteria

Separate three questions:

1. **Dynamic crossing:** body pitch >=45°, positive pitch rate, both front feet
   loaded at the crossing, rear feet unloaded, and no earlier native-state or
   collision violation. No low-speed condition or hold at 45° is required.
2. **Supported progression for a pilot:** continue toward 60° (at least 50°),
   survive at least .15 s after crossing, and retain the original native torque,
   speed and joint limits throughout the .8-second horizon with no nonfoot/self
   collision. Retained support safeguards are front-contact gap <=.02 s,
   foot-center motion integral <=.02 m and front displacement <=.04 m.
3. **Separate recovery:** record a 60° ±5° low-speed endpoint, pitch speed <=.5
   rad/s, COM speed <.75 m/s and both front feet supported for .15 s. This is a
   short endpoint diagnostic, not a 45° crossing gate or a successful handstand.
   The original five-second upright balance objective is not relaxed.

An exploratory 10–20-update pilot requires supported progression in >=8/10
fresh resets for each source seed. A mere collision-free peak is insufficient.
Recovery is tracked separately and is required before any extended-training
recommendation. Neither support nor recovery passes here. Motion measures use
the previous conservative foot-center proxies; they do not change friction or
any physical contact geometry. The observed displacements are vastly above the
proxy thresholds, so this is not a borderline tolerance rejection.

The earlier **45.1909°** sequence passes the new instantaneous crossing test:

## Focused full-MuJoCo search

The search uses fixed-geometry update-30 actors for seeds 42/43 from
`output/multimorph_bc_ppo_continuation_20261111_v2/A_seed*/checkpoint_000030.pt`.
These are the existing BC-initialized policies associated with the prior best
45.19° optimization. This lineage differs from the later update-36 global
benchmark; it is stated explicitly rather than mixing reset or checkpoint
results as if they measured learning.

Start states are generated by genuine source-policy standing rollouts through
certified takeoff. No pose/velocity injection or external impulse is used. Both
actors retain 44 observations, 12 actions, native joint envelopes, gains 60/3,
action scale 2 and target filter 1. Original policy weights are never updated.

Development resets: **61000, 61002, 61003**. Fresh validation: **67200–67209**.
The latter are not used for optimization. Four time blocks × six grouped
front/rear joint-type residuals yield 24 search parameters. The actor plus
bounded residual produces actual native joint-target actions. Existing best
residuals initialize the search. Four generations of 24 candidates are evaluated
on three development resets per seed: **576 full-MuJoCo development cases**.
The horizon is .8 seconds; invalid cases terminate early.

The first two generations explore with a smaller failure penalty; the final
objective and all acceptance tests always apply the strict physical/support
criteria. No gradient through contact or unconstrained pendulum torque is used.
The final safety score prefers settling controls, so dynamic-progress candidates
are also examined separately instead of being hidden behind those controls.

Their actions were recovered deterministically from the saved CEM seed and
rankings, then reproduced on all three original development cases. Peak pitch
matches within 1e-6 rad. No second optimization or broad diagnostic campaign was
run. Dynamic candidates are selected by development progression only, then
tested on the fresh reset family. These additional frontier tests are exploratory.

and unrecovered angular motion.

## Pendulum interpretation

COM angle and rate are computed relative to the projected front-foot midpoint,
independently of body pitch. With theta from upward vertical:

```
J = I_COM,y + m*l²
K_useful = .5*J*max(theta_dot,0)²  # while COM is behind support
barrier = m*g*(l-z)
coast_required_theta_dot = sqrt(2*barrier/J)
radial_acceleration_term = -2*(l_dot/l)*theta_dot
```

`K_useful` counts only the kinetic component directed toward COM-over-support;
it is not total rotational kinetic energy. The coast estimate assumes a frozen
length/support and no later actuator work, and is not a body-rate target or an
exact articulated reachability test. Rapidly moving/lost front contacts violate
those assumptions. Actual feasibility is decided by MuJoCo, not this estimate.

In the new collision-free sequence, measured relative COM rate at the 45° body
crossing is **−1.066 to −.657 rad/s**, despite positive body rate. The conditional
coast requirement is approximately **+.91 to +1.14 rad/s**, with useful-energy
deficit about **1.18–1.86 J**. The old 45.19° sequence similarly has COM rate
−.793 rad/s and a much larger conditional deficit. These values show why body
pitch progression cannot be equated with progress of the COM pendulum. They do
not justify injecting more motor torque indiscriminately; articulated shape
changes and moving support dominate this regime. Shortening amplifies existing
angular motion and can amplify motion in the wrong direction as well.

## Pilot preparation and stop decision

`frontier/pilot_preparation.json` records a **disabled** 10-update plan, capped at
20, with seeds 42/43, the existing actor/critic/Adam initialization, original BC
prefix anchor at coefficient 2, learning rate 1e-5 and 1,024 transitions/update.
The existing `RotationRun`, its reward/environment, architecture and actuator
interface are reused. No environment rebuild, new controller or CPG is needed.
These exploratory unstable sequences are not silently inserted as imitation
demonstrations.

`run_dynamic_rotation_pilot.py` defaults to preparation only. Even `--execute`
checks native/support success across ten resets per seed before loading an actor
or constructing training environments. The local rejection test records
`actor_loaded=false`, `ppo_interactions=0`. A forged success flag with the observed
support loss is rejected by a unit test. Full completion of a future 10-update
pilot would use 20,480 PPO interactions across two seeds (40,960 for 20 updates).
There is no automatic extended run.

**Molab recommendation:** no PPO yet. Go only for a further bounded contact-
recovery/clearance refinement. Verified safe settling controls exist below the
target; the 51° moving-support sequence is not a stationary handstand reference.
Next alternatives to test are coordinated front-thigh/calf clearance and rear
retraction, a terminal posture with COM over useful front support, or an explicitly
separate controlled-step/recontact task. These are proposals, not certified new
maneuvers. Do not silently relax friction, torque limits, or collisions.
